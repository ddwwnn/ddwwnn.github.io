Reviewable Dividend Event QA Pipeline

This note describes a dividend event ingestion and QA workflow for imperfect corporate action datasets.

The underlying project, divpipe, is concerned with the treatment of uncertain dividend observations where automatic downstream use would require a stronger evidential basis.

Dividend observations are not always suitable for direct downstream use, and automatic cleaning is not, without an adequate evidential basis, a sufficient control. Missing data is usually visible; a wrongly merged event, duplicated cash flow or discarded observation is often not.

The project therefore treats uncertain observations as reviewable evidence: they are retained, classified and routed so that operator judgement can be replayed against the same inputs rather than being embedded silently in a downstream file.

The issue

Dividend event data is not a tidy control surface.

A provider row may record a genuine dividend event. It may also record a correction, an ex-date movement, a repeated amount, an instalment pattern, a currency representation issue or a market-specific reporting convention.

The relevant risk is not confined to visibly broken records. Identical amounts do not establish duplication. Different dates do not establish separate events. A valid ticker may have no observed dividend history in the requested window; another may exist but remain coverage-limited under the available provider surface.

A pipeline that deduplicates before preserving the underlying evidence does not provide a reliable control. The evidence must remain available for review before downstream reliance is permitted.

Design position

The treatment is deliberately conservative:

Ambiguity should remain visible unless there is a sufficient basis for automatic resolution.

divpipe separates provider observations from economic event identity. A provider observation is a row received or derived from a data surface. An economic event is the underlying dividend event that one or more observations may be describing.

That separation is material. Several observations may refer to the same economic event, while a single economic event may appear with conflicting dates, amounts or currencies.

Where the evidence is insufficient, the appropriate treatment is not finalisation. divpipe links the observations, builds economic-event level signals and routes the case into an explicit QA surface.

v1 structure

The v1 workflow is organised around three stages.

flowchart LR A[iShares raw JSON] --> B[iShares normalise] B --> C[ETF holdings input] C --> D[Stage 1 ingest] D --> E[Canonical seed artefacts] E --> F[Event linking] F --> G[Severity model] G --> H[QA queues] I[Operator overrides] --> G G --> J[Replayable outputs]

The routing view is intentionally shown as a horizontal control flow.

Stage 0: holdings preparation

Stage 0 prepares holdings inputs from iShares raw JSON snapshots.

At this stage, the relevant control is provenance and reproducibility: the workflow treats the raw snapshot as the source record, avoids manually repaired mapping files and produces a normalised holdings file under a fixed input contract for Stage 1.

Stage 1: provider ingestion

Stage 1 records dividend observations from the public provider surface and writes canonical seed artefacts.

Ticker-resolution outcomes are retained as part of the evidential record, so that failed, ambiguous or coverage-limited cases remain separately identifiable through ticker reports, no-dividend triage files and candidate-resolution queues rather than being collapsed into a generic error category.

For v1, the provider response is not treated as operationally sufficient merely because a fetch completed; the corresponding resolution outcome must also remain available for review.

Stage 2: severity routing

Stage 2 groups dividend observations by economic event, derives review signals and assigns severity tiers.

The stage does not purport to resolve all event ambiguity automatically. Its function is to determine, on the available evidence, whether an event has a sufficient basis for use within the current policy boundary or whether it should remain subject to review.

Tier 0 and Tier 1 cases remain review-required, while Tier 2+ cases may be used only within the current policy boundary.

Operator decisions are applied by replay, so that rows may be excluded, economic event ids may be set and anchor dates may be amended before Stage 2 is rerun against the same input artefacts.

Why automatic deduplication is insufficient

Automatic deduplication is not, by itself, a sufficient control for dividend observations.

An amount-based key may group together events that are economically separate. A date-based key may fail where the same economic event is amended across provider updates. A currency-based key may overlook mixed-currency representations of the same underlying event.

The relevant question is therefore not whether two rows can be made to match under a convenient key, but whether there is a sufficient evidential basis to treat them as the same economic event for downstream use.

divpipe treats same-date amount collisions, ex-date movement, mixed-currency representation and vendor identity conflict as review conditions. They are routed through severity logic so that the underlying observations remain available for review rather than being absorbed into a cleaned output table.

Operational value

The output of divpipe is not just a final CSV, but the control surface around the data:

  • canonical Stage 1 seed artefacts
  • ticker-resolution reports
  • no-dividend triage files
  • linked row-level severity outputs
  • economic-event summaries
  • Tier 0 and Tier 1 QA queues
  • replayable override decisions
  • run metadata for audit and debugging

Downstream reliance is supported only where the underlying observations have been retained, classified and assessed against the relevant policy boundary.

Where that basis is absent, the matter would ordinarily remain subject to review rather than being silently normalised.

Current validation snapshot:

  • Test suite: 221 pytest cases passing
  • Schema contracts: enforced for Stage 1 and Stage 2 artefacts
  • Replay path: Stage 2 can be rerun from fixed Stage 1 seed artefacts
  • Guardrail: --max-tier0-ratio active for Tier 0 concentration checks

Scope

divpipe v1 should not be read as a golden-source corporate actions engine, nor as a representation of complete venue or provider coverage.

The outputs remain subject to the policy boundaries of the current implementation. Where a provider surface is unsupported, coverage-limited or otherwise unresolved, the relevant observation is retained as a reviewable case unless there is a sufficient evidential basis for automatic downstream use.

Accordingly, the scope is limited to reproducible ingestion, explicit review surfaces and controlled treatment of imperfect observations.

Design conclusion

Provider access and CSV generation are implementation details unless the pipeline also defines the boundary of trust: which observations may be consumed, which must be rejected, which require review and where downstream reliance should be withheld.

divpipe makes that boundary explicit by retaining uncertain dividend observations as reviewable evidence and by routing them through reproducible control surfaces rather than converting them into a falsely complete downstream file.
